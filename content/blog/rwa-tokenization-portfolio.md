Real-world assets are one of the most useful ideas in DeFi and one of the easiest to misread. A token can give you exposure to something real without giving you the thing itself.

This page explains RWA tokenization and portfolio oversight as the Olive product describes them, with the terms that matter kept visible.

## What RWA tokenization means

Tokenizing a real-world asset means putting an interest in that asset into a token. The token can then be held, transferred or used in a product that supports it.

The word interest is doing a lot of work. A token may represent a legal interest, a debt claim, a beneficial interest, or a synthetic exposure. Those are not the same thing.

That is why the product copy states the rule plainly: access is not ownership.

## What a token may represent

| Structure | What the holder may have |
| --- | --- |
| Legal interest | A claim connected to the underlying asset or entity |
| Debt | A right to repayment under the product terms |
| Beneficial interest | A beneficial right held through a structure |
| Synthetic | An exposure that tracks a value without direct ownership |

The table is a map, not a promise. The actual terms come from the issuer and the product, not from the token symbol. Read them before you rely on the label.

## The terms to read before you hold

Six terms decide what an RWA product is.

1. **Issuer.** Who stands behind the product.
2. **Structure.** What the token legally represents.
3. **Income.** Whether there is any, and how it is paid.
4. **Valuation.** How the value is reported and how often.
5. **Liquidity.** Whether you can exit, and under what conditions.
6. **Redemption.** How the holding ends and what you receive.

If a product does not answer those, it is not ready to hold. A clean interface is not a substitute for terms.

## Why access is not ownership

Access means you can reach a product through a platform. Ownership means a legal relationship to the underlying asset.

A platform can provide access without becoming the issuer or the owner. The product copy is explicit about this: RWA products are third-party, product-specific investments, and the terms vary by issuer and infrastructure.

That distinction changes what you are relying on. You are relying on the issuer and the structure, not on the platform's interface.

## What portfolio oversight means

Oversight is the other half. Holding an RWA is not a one-time action. It has a lifecycle.

The Olive description groups oversight into three capabilities.

- **Unified portfolio.** Supported assets and positions in one view.
- **Capital and exposure.** Allocation, positions and performance context.
- **RWA lifecycle.** Income, valuation, maturity and redemption terms.

The flow is consolidate, allocate, monitor. The product copy notes that no instant liquidation or automatic rebalancing is implied.

That is a useful warning. A portfolio view can look more liquid than the assets inside it.

## Where the risk sits

RWA risk is mostly in the terms and the structure.

- **Issuer risk.** The party behind the product may not perform.
- **Valuation risk.** Reported values may lag the market or be hard to verify.
- **Liquidity risk.** Exit may be limited or product-specific.
- **Structural risk.** The legal interest may be weaker than the label suggests.
- **Operational risk.** Lifecycle events can be missed if oversight is weak.

Oversight helps with the last one. It does not remove the first four.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a tokenized RWA mean I own the asset?

Not necessarily. A token may represent a legal, debt, beneficial or synthetic interest. Read the terms.

### Are RWA returns guaranteed?

No. Income and performance are product-specific and not guaranteed.

### Can I always sell an RWA token?

No. Liquidity is product-specific. Do not assume instant exit.

### What does oversight add?

A view of positions, exposure, reported valuations and lifecycle events. It does not remove issuer or liquidity risk.
