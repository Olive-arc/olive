Agentic trading vs copy trading is a question about control. Two products are often described as automated trading, but they are not the same. Copy trading follows a person. Agentic trading follows a rule set you define.

The difference sounds small until something goes wrong, and then it decides who is responsible.

## Copy trading, briefly

Copy trading lets you mirror another trader's positions. You choose an account, set an amount, and the system repeats their trades in yours.

The control you keep is the choice of who to follow and how much to allocate. The control you give up is the reasoning. You may not know why a position was opened, and you may not know when it will close.

Copy trading can be transparent about positions and opaque about intent.

## Agentic trading, briefly

Agentic trading uses authorised agents that act within a strategy and limits you assign. You configure objectives, assets, risk and capital limits. The agent executes only inside that mandate.

The control you keep is the mandate. You can review activity, adjust the limits, or revoke authority.

The description is specific for a reason. The product is about bounded execution, not about following a personality.

## The comparison

| Question | Copy trading | Agentic trading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What you choose | A trader to follow | A mandate you define |
| What decides the action | The other trader | The agent, inside your limits |
| What you can inspect | Their positions, if disclosed | Activity, positions and permissions |
| What you can change | Who you follow and how much | Objectives, limits and authority |
| Main risk | Their decisions are not yours | A permitted but unintended action |
| Main control | Allocation | Mandate and revocation |

The table is the whole argument. Both systems automate action. Only one makes the rule set yours.

## Where copy trading is weaker

Three weaknesses.

**Intent is hidden.** You see positions, not the thesis. When the trader changes their mind, you may not know why.

**Style drift.** The trader may change strategy, and your allocation follows without a new decision from you.

**Accountability is split.** If a copied trade loses money, the decision was theirs and the loss is yours.

None of this makes copy trading wrong. It makes it a bet on a person.

## Where agentic trading is weaker

Three weaknesses, stated plainly.

**The mandate can be underspecified.** "Grow the account" is not a limit. A vague mandate gives the agent room to do something permitted and unintended.

**The agent can be wrong within its limits.** A bounded action is still an action. The boundary reduces the set of mistakes. It does not remove them.

**Oversight takes work.** A mandate that is set and forgotten drifts as conditions change. Review is part of the product.

## What to check in either model

1. What can act without a new approval from me?
2. What limits are enforced rather than merely described?
3. What can I inspect, and how often?
4. How do I stop it, and how fast does the stop take effect?
5. What happens when a limit is hit mid-action?

If the answer is "the system is careful", keep asking. Care is not a control.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is agentic trading the same as copy trading?

No. Copy trading mirrors a person. Agentic trading follows limits you define.

### Which gives me more control?

Agentic trading gives you control over the mandate. Copy trading gives you control over who you follow and how much.

### Is either one safer?

Neither is safer by default. The risks sit in different places.

### Can I stop an agent?

Yes. The product description says authority can be revoked. Ask how fast the stop takes effect before you rely on it.
